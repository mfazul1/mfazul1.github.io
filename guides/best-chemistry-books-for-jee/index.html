Best Chemistry Books for JEE Main & Advanced

Updated for JEE Main & Advanced preparation · JEE IITian Books

Chemistry is the most scoreable of the three subjects because its three branches behave completely differently — and the best books differ for each. Physical chemistry rewards numerical practice, organic chemistry rewards reaction-mechanism thinking, and inorganic chemistry rewards memory of NCERT. If you treat them as one subject and one book, you will underserve two of them. This guide breaks the best books down by branch and shows you how to split your effort.

Physical Chemistry

What it needs

Physical chemistry is mathematics in a lab coat. Equilibrium, thermodynamics, kinetics, electrochemistry, and solutions all come down to applying formulas and units correctly. The skill you are building is numerical fluency — recognising which equation a question wants, converting units without error, and finishing calculations quickly.

Best books

  • N. Awasthi — strong on theory with a clean progression of problems; a solid first companion after NCERT.
  • P. Bahadur / Physical Chemistry — heavy on the type of numericals that appear in JEE Main, good for practice volume.
  • RC Mukherjee — more advanced and demanding, best once the basics are automatic and you are aiming for the Advanced-level physical questions.

For Main, one of Awasthi or Bahadur plus NCERT is enough. Add RC Mukherjee only if you need the harder edge for Advanced or you find the Main-level numericals too easy.

Organic Chemistry

What it needs

Organic chemistry is not memory of named reactions — it is mechanistic reasoning. If you understand why a nucleophile attacks here and not there, most reactions stop needing to be memorised individually. The skills that matter are arrow-pushing, stability arguments, and recognising the same mechanism hidden in unfamiliar molecules.

Best books

  • NCERT first — the reaction tables and named reactions in NCERT are the baseline for both Main and Advanced.
  • M.S. Chauhan / OP Tandon — excellent for organised reaction coverage and mechanism practice; the two are often used together to build both theory and questions.
  • Advanced extras — a mechanism-heavy problem source or additional reaction practice becomes valuable once NCERT and one of the above are done, because Advanced frequently tests unusual applications of standard mechanisms.

Build your organic study around mechanism practice: name every reaction, then ask yourself what would happen if the reagents changed. That habit scores far better than memorising reaction lists.

Inorganic Chemistry

What it needs

Inorganic chemistry in JEE Main is largely NCERT-based. A large share of the questions come straight from the NCERT texts on s-, p-, d- and f-block elements, coordination compounds, and the periodic table. The winning skill is faithful reading and revision of NCERT, supported by selective practice to lock in exceptions and trends.

Best books

  • NCERT + NCERT Supplementary — non-negotiable. Read these line by line, repeatedly, because Main pulls directly from them.
  • A practice companion — use a general practice book or PYQ section for inorganic to test whether you have truly absorbed the NCERT facts and their subtle exceptions.

Do not over-collect inorganic books. The student who rereads NCERT until every line is familiar, then tests themselves with PYQs, outperforms the student with five inorganic reference books.

PYQ question banks

Why they matter

Previous-year questions are the most exam-accurate practice in all of chemistry, because examiners reuse the same ideas, exceptions, and traps year after year. Books like the Disha 48 years chemistry bank collect decades of questions by chapter, which lets you see exactly what the paper rewards.

How to use them

  • Do PYQs by chapter as you finish each topic — they reveal the real difficulty and the recurring patterns.
  • Later, do complete papers under timed conditions to build speed and composure.
  • Every PYQ you get wrong is a flag for a specific, patchable weakness — not a reason to panic.

A per-topic study strategy

Physical

Learn the concept from one book, then solve numericals daily. Physical chemistry decays fastest without practice, so it belongs in every study session, even if only for twenty minutes.

Organic

Study in mechanism families rather than reaction lists. After learning a reaction, immediately attempt the variation questions that flip the reagents. Revise the NCERT named reactions regularly.

Inorganic

Own NCERT. Read a chapter, make a one-page sheet of the exceptions and trends, then verify yourself with PYQs. This branch needs repeated, spaced revision far more than it needs a big book.

How to split your time

  1. Daily: solve a few physical numericals to keep the math warm.
  2. Weekly: cover one organic mechanism family with variation questions.
  3. Spaced: reread an NCERT inorganic chapter every month on a revision calendar.
  4. Exam season: switch almost entirely to PYQs by chapter, then full timed papers.

Our verdict

Buy physical and organic books, but let NCERT run inorganic for you. A realistic stack is one physical book (Awasthi or Bahadur), one organic book (M.S. Chauhan / OP Tandon), NCERT as the backbone, and a PYQ bank to test everything. Resist the urge to gather a huge shelf — chemistry rewards targeted, practised depth far more than breadth.
