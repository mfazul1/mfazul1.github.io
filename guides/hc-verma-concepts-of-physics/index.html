HC Verma Concepts of Physics — Review for JEE

Updated for JEE Main & Advanced preparation · JEE IITian Books

What this book teaches

Concepts of Physics (Volumes 1 and 2) is HC Verma's famous two-volume text that explains the core ideas of physics first and applies them to problems second. It does not follow the NCERT chapter order exactly, but covers all topics relevant to JEE: mechanics, heat and thermodynamics, waves, electromagnetism, optics, and modern physics.

The unique value of the book is its conceptual-first approach. Each chapter begins with a clear explanation of the underlying idea, builds the necessary mathematics, then works through "objective problems" and "exercises" graded from easy to difficult.

Who should use it

  • Class 11 students who want to build the right physics foundation from the start.
  • Class 12 / dropper students who struggle with the "why" behind formulas and need concepts explained clearly.
  • Any aspirant who finds that memorising formulas alone is not enough for JEE-style application questions.

JEE Main vs JEE Advanced suitability

ExamSuitabilityWhy
JEE MainHighCovers concepts behind NCERT-level and Main-level questions with clarity.
JEE AdvancedMedium-High (foundation)Great for understanding, but Advanced-level problem practice needs an additional problem book.

Difficulty level

Concept explanations are accessible to a beginner, but the exercise sets climb steeply. Theoretically "easy to read," practically demanding — most students need a teacher or topper notes to move through the exercises efficiently.

Important chapters

  • Volume 1: Laws of Motion, Work & Energy, Rotational Motion, Gravitation, Properties of Matter, Simple Harmonic Motion.
  • Volume 2: Electrostatics, Current Electricity, Magnetic Effects, Electromagnetic Induction, Ray & Wave Optics, Modern Physics.

How to study from the book

  1. Read the theory section of one chapter carefully before touching the problems.
  2. Reproduce the derivations yourself — this fixes the concepts in memory.
  3. Attempt the "objective I and II" questions for quick JEE applicability.
  4. Then solve the "exercises," marking every question you could not solve.
  5. Revise the marked questions after 7 days and again before your mock tests.

Recommended sequence

Use Volume 1 during Class 11 and Volume 2 during Class 12. Pair each chapter with your school/coaching schedule so the book reinforces what you learn in class rather than racing ahead of it.

Strengths

  • Exceptional clarity of concepts — the best at building genuine understanding.
  • Graded problems that teach you how to think, not just plug values.
  • An absolute classic recommended across generations of toppers.

Limitations

  • Not enough advanced-level numerical problems for JEE Advanced after ~2020 pattern shifts.
  • Some topics overlap awkwardly with the NCERT syllabus order.
  • Can feel slow if you use it as your only problem source.

Alternative books

  • DC Pandey series — for higher problem volume and exam-style questions.
  • IE Irodov — for a serious challenge beyond JEE Advanced level.
  • Cengage Physics (G. Tewani) — for structured theory plus advanced practice.

Our verdict

Start with HC Verma if you are building your physics foundation. It is the single best book for understanding physics for JEE. Once concepts are solid, add a dedicated problem/PYQ book for exam-style practice and JEE Advanced difficulty. Treat HC Verma as the foundation layer, not the only layer.
